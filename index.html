    One phone, passed around. Saying a term out loud and explaining it is one of the best ways to remember it.

    How it works
    1. Pick what you're revising: one subject or several, the topics you've covered, and how deep to go.
    2. Flashcards use spaced repetition. Say the answer out loud, flip, and be honest: cards you know come back after 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; cards you don't come back today. A term counts as mastered from the 7-day stage.
    3. Practice questions are single best answer, untimed, with the explanation straight after each one. Wrong answers go on your revision pile.
    4. 30 Seconds: one person describes five terms to their team without saying them. When time's up, read every term's fact out loud. Missed terms go on the pile and come up in your flashcards.
    5. Everything is saved on this phone. Nothing to sign up for.

      The rules
      1. On each turn, one person describes and the rest of their team guesses.
      2. The describer gets five terms and 30 seconds to get the team to say them, in any order.
      3. No saying the term or any part of it, no abbreviations of it, no "sounds like", no spelling it out.
      4. Tap a term, or swipe it right, the moment your team gets it. Cleared all five with time left? Deal the next card.
      5. When time's up, read every term's fact out loud: that's the studying. Missed terms go on the revision pile.
      6. One point per term guessed. First team to the target wins.

                        How the quiz works
                        1. Each turn, spin for a category (spot diagnosis, investigations, management, pharmacology…), then answer one question with four options.
                        2. A right answer is worth 500, plus up to 500 more the faster you lock it in.
                        3. Streaks pay: +100 for each right answer in a row after the first, up to +500.
                        4. Each team has one 50:50. It knocks out two wrong answers.
                        5. The last round is worth double. Questions get harder as the rounds go on.
                        6. Every wrong answer goes on your revision pile, with the explanation.
